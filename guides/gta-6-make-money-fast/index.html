How to Make Money Fast in GTA 6, From Chapter 1 to Bank Vaults

How to make money fast in GTA 6: the best early and mid-game earners by chapter, typical payouts, what to buy first, and the money mistakes to avoid.

Updated Nov 24, 20267 min read17 sources checked
QUICK ANSWER

Early on, raid Illegal Operations (yellow map icons), fence what you find and, once Chapter 2 opens, sell tracker-free stolen cars to Moses. From Chapter 3, jewelry store robberies ($10,000 to $25,000) and Randy's wish-list cars ($30,000 or more) are the big earners, and bank vault robberies (up to about $200,000) top everything near the end of the story. Deposit your cash as you go, because being Wasted or Busted can cost you the money you're carrying.

Why money matters more in GTA 6

Cash isn't just for toys this time. Some story missions won't move forward until you can pay your way, and Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told IGN, "We have made money matter more in this story." He also told Famitsu that mission payouts alone won't cover what the story asks for, so side income is part of the plan.

There's no real-money shortcut either, because Nelson has also said GTA 6 has no monetization. Your money sits in three places, and knowing the difference is the first step to keeping it.

How to make money early in GTA 6 (Chapters 1 and 2)

Chapter 1 heads-up: don't grind. PowerPyx and Dexerto both report that the money you're holding when Chapter 1 ends doesn't come with you, while Family Savings deposits do. Earn what you need, put spare cash into savings, and keep the story moving.

Your best Chapter 1 earner is Illegal Operations. VICE and Dexerto both point to the Exotic Animals Front hidden behind the Three Sheets liquor store in Key Lento, plus Kingfisher hideouts in the swamps nearby. Take the cash and the narcotics, then sell the drugs to Brian or Donnie. Brian's lockup becomes your first fence during the mission Lab Fees.

After Island Dreams, Kenton in Key Lento offers treasure diving around the Keys. It's low risk, but Kenton only pays once he has sold the haul, so expect the money later.

In Chapter 2, Moses opens up car selling, which quickly becomes your bread and butter:

  1. Pull out your phone, open the WAiNK app and scan a car. It shows the sale value, which buyers want it, its condition and its security.
  2. Pick a car with no tracker. Moses refuses anything tracked, but locked doors are fine once he shows you how to jimmy them.
  3. Steal it a few streets away. Moses won't take cars swiped too close to his garage, and the main road through Port Gellhorn is a good hunting ground.
  4. Drive gently. Damage lowers the payout.
  5. Sell, then wait out his short cooldown before bringing the next car.

Best mid-game money makers (Chapter 3 onward)

Chapter 3 is where the money really opens up, because new robbery types and better-paying buyers unlock through the story.

  • Jewelry stores: Yasmani's Jewelry Jobs introduce these, and each robbery is worth roughly $10,000 to $25,000. Open the back-room safe too, then sell the jewelry to Yasmani or a fence before anything goes wrong.
  • Randy's Wish List: the Key Cloner from Clonin' Keys, Makin' Gs lets you take high-end cars with advanced trackers, and Randy pays $30,000 or more for wish-list cars in perfect condition. Check your phone's Reminders app to see what he wants.
  • Pay 'n' Spray sales: from early Chapter 3 these shops also buy cars with standard trackers, around $4,000 for a good one, but they cap how many they take per day.
  • Scout before you strike: the Robberies section of the map index lists Smash 'n' Grabs, banks and payday loan targets, and hovering over one shows the potential score.
  • Shop your loot around: fences rate goods from Very Low to Very High, so the same bag pays differently depending on where you sell it, and with some fences the money takes days to reach your mailbox.
  • Bank vaults: near the end of Chapter 4, the optional quest That's Where The Money Is… teaches vault robberies worth up to about $200,000. PowerPyx's method is to tail a security van to a bank, slip into the vault while it's open, then outrun the heavy police response on a fast motorcycle.
Tip

Bring your partner on big jobs. They can carry extra loot bags, and you can send them to fence the goods for you. Rockstar's November 23 update fixed a bug that stopped partners from handing over bags to sell.

What to spend money on first in GTA 6

Spend on things that make the next score easier or protect what you've already earned. A sensible order:

  1. Family Savings goals. Per PowerPyx, the first $5,000 goal raises your checking cap to $15,000, and the $10,000 goal lifts it to $100,000 and takes 20% off RydeMe rides. Only deposit money you won't need, because it never comes back out.
  2. Weapon upgrades, armor and healing. A suppressor lets you clear gang hideouts quietly, before the police get involved.
  3. A registered getaway car. Moses, and later Pay 'n' Spray shops, can register a stolen car as your personal vehicle so you keep it.
  4. Cheap disguises. A hat, glasses or a quick outfit change helps you shake off heat after a job.
  5. Garages, once the money flows. You buy them through the Vice City Real Estate app, and no income-earning businesses for sale have turned up so far.
  6. A winch from a Mod Shop. GamesRadar+ notes it lets you rip ATMs out for extra cash.
Tip

Hold off on luxury cars and cosmetic car upgrades early. They look great but don't help you earn or survive.

GTA 6 money mistakes to avoid

  • Carrying a fortune. Being Wasted or Busted can wipe out the cash in your pocket, so deposit it. The ATM Finder in the BuckMe app marks the nearest machine.
  • Grinding in Chapter 1. Whatever you hold when the chapter ends won't carry over, apart from Family Savings.
  • Overfilling Family Savings. It's permanent, so keep enough in checking for mission costs.
  • Ignoring your checking cap. Once it's full, payments sit as pending until you spend or save.
  • Selling tracked or banged-up cars. Scan with WAiNK first, steal away from the buyer and avoid crashes on the way.
  • Dumping everything at one fence. Different fences pay better for different goods.
  • Expecting instant payouts. Fence money and Kenton's treasure cash arrive later.
  • Banking on the money cheat. It only unlocks after the story, and cheats switch off saving, so the $250,000 is gone when you quit.

GTA 6 money guide FAQ

What is the fastest way to make money in GTA 6?

Large bank vault robberies, which pay up to about $200,000 but only open near the end of Chapter 4. Before that, Randy's wish-list cars ($30,000 or more) and jewelry store robberies ($10,000 to $25,000) in Chapter 3 are the best earners.

Is there a money cheat in GTA 6?

Yes. Up, Right, Down, Left, Up, Left, Down, Right gives you $250,000, but only after you finish the story, and using it disables saving, so you can't keep the cash.

Do you lose money when you die in GTA 6?

Pocket cash is the money at risk when you're Wasted or Busted. GamesRadar+ and PowerPyx say you lose all of it, while other guides report smaller losses, so deposit big amounts. Family Savings can never be lost.

How much cash can you carry in GTA 6?

Jason and Lucia can each carry up to $20,000 in pocket cash. ATMs let you deposit or withdraw up to $20,000 at a time.

Can you buy businesses in GTA 6?

Not so far. Guides covering the released game have found no income-generating businesses; garages are the only property you can buy, and safehouses come with the story.

Can you buy GTA 6 money with real money?

No. Rockstar North's Rob Nelson said GTA 6 has no monetization, so every dollar has to be earned in the game.

Why won't Moses buy my car?

Moses rejects cars with trackers and cars stolen too close to his garage, and he needs a short break between sales. Scan cars with the WAiNK app first and keep them undamaged for the best price.

Can you get stuck in the story without money?

Yes. Some missions need cash before they move forward, so keep a buffer in your checking account and top it up with car sales and robberies.
